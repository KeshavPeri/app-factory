## 5. Repo facts worth knowing before you touch anything

- `code` is the stable cross-season key. **Never** `player_id` or `teams.id` — FPL element ids are not stable across seasons (`deltas.md` D9).
- `player_match_stats` contains all competitions — filter `competition = 'prem'`.
- `goals_conceded` is a **goalkeeper** stat. Use `team_goals_conceded`, and take the **MAX across a club's players in a match**, never the average or first row.
- Supabase caps queries at 1,000 rows. `scripts/lib/paginate.ts` fails closed without an explicit ordering.
- RLS and GRANTs are independent gates (`deltas.md` D8).
- `player_gameweek_history.now_cost` is **DECIMAL MILLIONS** (e.g. `5.8`). `public.players.now_cost` and FPL's API are **integer tenths**. Not interchangeable — mixing them misprices by 10×.
- `player_gameweek_history.bonus`, `.bps`, `.starts` are **season-cumulative-to-date**, not per-gameweek. Difference consecutive rows.
- `deltas.md` D11: never substitute another provider's Elo — scale mismatch.
- Device VM limits: `npm test`/vitest cannot run there (native binding mismatch); `gh` is not installed there; git has no identity there; the proxy blocks `api.clubelo.com` and `fantasy.premierleague.com` — Keshav runs those curls himself.

## 7. WHY THIS HAS BEEN GOING IN CIRCLES — my honest account

Roughly 35 tickets have gone into the projection model. The measured result: a one-gameweek Spearman of ~0.354 against a naive minutes baseline of ~0.345, and a five-gameweek Spearman of 0.409 against a naive 0.407. **The model barely beats "assume players repeat their minutes per match."** That is the uncomfortable truth the new session should start from.

What went wrong, as patterns:

**1. Instrument defects outnumbered model defects, three waves running.** Most tickets fixed measurement, not the model. Four separate lookahead leaks in the backtest harness. A stale team-rating table nobody noticed for four gameweeks. A source file that silently published two blank columns. Every one of these was real and worth fixing — but the cumulative effect is that we spent 35 runs building a measuring apparatus and very little time improving predictions.

**2. Falsification gates kept failing in new ways, and I wrote most of those gates.**
- §20: a gate whose only instrument was a `workflow_dispatch` Action no Builder could trigger.
- §21: a gate comparing new-against-old, which passes **vacuously** when the new path never runs — #229 shipped completely dead code and reported PASS.
- A **one-sided** bound ("spread must not be lower") that passed a 74% over-dispersion.
- A gate anchored to a **dated example** (Man Utd v Man City in GW4) that had expired by merge time.
- Three tickets blocked because I put live-data steps in the Definition of Done.
Each fix was right. But four consecutive gate-design failures means the ticket format itself was the bottleneck, not the model.

**3. We rebuilt football modelling from scratch.** Elo from results, shrinkage constants, an attacking multiplier, a defensive multiplier, a bonus allocator, a BPS estimator — each one derived and calibrated in-house over multiple tickets. Almost none of it was checked against how FPL modellers actually do this. The single biggest improvement of the whole project — market odds — came from one afternoon of web research, not from 35 tickets of first-principles derivation. **That is the lesson. Research before building.**

**4. Substrate tickets begat substrate tickets.** Ingest price history → now we can replay → but the replay needs another ticket → which needs the scorecard corrected → and so on. Keshav named this explicitly and is right.

**5. The recommendation layer — the actual product — has never been measured.** Everything measured stops at the projection. Whether the transfers and captaincy are any good is still essentially unknown after 35 runs. Plan A beating Roll by 5 points in 4 gameweeks is the only signal, and it is noise.

**What I would tell the next session to do differently:** research first, prefer a known-good external signal over an in-house derivation, measure the *decision* not the *projection*, and never write a ticket whose deliverable is another instrument unless it directly unblocks a decision change in the very next ticket.

### Model findings worth carrying
- **ClubElo is abandoned, not down.** `clubelo.com/ENG` serves data dated 22 Oct 2024; the API 502s; the best daily mirror (`tonyelhabr/club-rankings`) died 2026-01-14. Do not go looking again.
- **FPL's own `strength_*` fields are useless.** Live check: `strength` null for all 20 clubs, all attack/defence values 0, `strength_overall_home/away` a 2–5 bucket giving Chelsea and Man Utd both 4/4.
- **`data/2026-2027/teams.csv` publishes `elo` AND `fotmob_name` blank for all 20 clubs.** Each blank column disabled a different part of the model. When a source degrades, assume more than one column went with it.
- **Market odds convert to expectedScore with no fitted parameter:** strip the overround, then `p_home + 0.5 × p_draw`. Free tier 500 req/month, ~30 used. Horizon ~24 days (about 4 gameweeks of the 5-gameweek projection).
- **Bonus allocator was too flat** — projected 0.224 vs actual 0.433 on the top 20. Fixed by an exponent on the BPS share (#241, re-fit on a full season by #122).
- **Defensive slope measured at −1.43 to −1.50**, not the model-implied −2.9. The attack/defence asymmetry was **not real**; every defender and goalkeeper in a good fixture had been over-projected all season.
- **#184's attacking damping is sound** — I wrongly suspected it was a stale-elo artefact; it was measured against point-in-time expectedScore all along.
- **`player_match_stats` two-stage shrinkage** (`SHRINKAGE_K = 3`) gives current-season form ~50% weight after 3 matches. Player form is handled; team strength had none until #242.
